About me

Bioinformatics with an evolutionary question.

I use computational genomics to study how plants adapt to challenging environments. My work combines genome annotation, protein prediction, orthology inference, BUSCO-based quality assessment, and downstream comparative analyses focused on candidate gene families and biological pathways.

Research profile

My current research is centered on arctic-alpine plant genomes, with a special interest in the CBF/cold-response pathway and its potential role in convergent adaptation to cold environments.

Computational approach

I build and test reproducible annotation workflows using tools such as GeneCAD, LiftOn, AGAT, BUSCO and OrthoFinder, running large-scale analyses on HPC clusters with SLURM.

Research interests

From raw genomes to evolutionary insight.

I am especially interested in how genome annotation quality affects downstream evolutionary inference, particularly in non-model species where reference resources are limited.

Selected projects

Current work.

These projects reflect my current focus on plant genome annotation, proteome validation, and comparative evolutionary analyses.

GeneCAD annotation of Cassiope tetragona

Built and validated a GeneCAD-based annotation workflow for a challenging non-model plant genome. The workflow included genome batching, GFF3 cleaning, CDS phase correction, protein extraction, internal-stop filtering and BUSCO validation.

77.6% complete BUSCOs 21k clean proteins 97% genes in orthogroups

Annotation comparison in Silene acaulis

Comparing LiftOn-derived and GeneCAD-derived proteomes to evaluate completeness, duplication, orthology behaviour and recovery of CBF/cold-response genes.

Silene acaulis LiftOn vs GeneCAD BUSCO comparison

CBF pathway convergence

Identifying and validating candidate CBF/cold-response pathway genes across arctic-alpine plant species to investigate potential signatures of convergent adaptation.

CBF Cold acclimation Convergence

Orthology pilot dataset

Building comparative proteome datasets with multiple diploid plant species and newly annotated arctic-alpine genomes to test gene family recovery and orthogroup assignment.
